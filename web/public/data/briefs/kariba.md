# Lake Kariba: brief for decision-makers
*Issued 15 Nov 2026. AI-written brief, checked against the Patronus data files (kariba.json, enso.json, trends.json). Not official advice.*

**Bottom line.** A very strong El Niño is under way, and Lake Kariba enters the rainy season at 31% usable storage. Patronus puts the chance of the lake falling below 10% usable storage by December 2027 at about **1 in 3 (36%)**, and below 20% at **45%**. Below 10%, the Zambezi River Authority cannot meet its allocation and generation is cut. That is what happened in 2024.

**What the data show now**
- Satellite altimetry (NASA/USDA G-REALM) reads 479.92 m on 9 Nov 2026, about 31% usable. The ZRA gauge read 479.98 m (31.7%) on 10 Nov.
- NOAA: El Niño Advisory, with a strong-to-very-strong El Niño likely through January–March 2027 (>79% chance). NASA's GEOS-S2S model forecasts Niño-3.4 at about +4 °C this season.
- In 34 years of satellite records, the lake rose **2.1 m less** from October to June in El Niño years than in other years (11 El Niño years, p = 0.01).
- ZRA has already cut the 2027 power allocation by 36%, to 23 billion m³. It projects 9% usable storage at the end of 2027 if the full allocation is used. 4 of the 11 past El Niño years in our record end below that level.

**Would Patronus have seen 2024 coming?** In a strictly forward backtest, the El Niño reservoir model first gave a better-than-even chance of the lake falling below 20% in **July 2023**. That is 5 months before ZRA cut the 2024 allocation (28 Dec 2023) and 8 months before Zambia's load-shedding began (11 Mar 2024). The July alarm came from already-low storage; from August 2023 the El Niño signal reinforced it (80% chance, Oct–Dec 2023). This model is not perfect: across El Niño seasons since 1995 it caught 3 crises, missed 2, and raised 2 false alarms.

**What waiting costs.** Zambia paid 17–26 US cents/kWh for emergency imports and diesel in 2024, about $51M a month for 300 MW (ZESCO filing to the regulator). Our water-balance model says acting in December rather than waiting until the lake crosses 20% saves money in most El Niño scenarios. However, the size of the saving depends strongly on assumptions, from about $8M to $144M. Treat the figure as illustrative.

**Options that are cheap now and expensive later**
- Lock in import contracts while prices are normal, instead of buying emergency power.
- Plan thermal and solar backup for the second half of 2027.
- Prepare load-shedding schedules early, so they can be phased in gently rather than imposed suddenly.

**Limits.** The ensemble has only 11 El Niño years, so its probabilities move in steps of about 9%. Altimetry and ZRA's gauge differ by up to 0.4 m at very low levels. The storage curve is fitted to five ZRA reference points. See the Proof page.
